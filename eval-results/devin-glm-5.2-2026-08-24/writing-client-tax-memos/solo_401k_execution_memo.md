## Rule

A one-participant 401(k) plan lets a business owner act in two capacities at
once: employee and employer. As the employee, Jordan makes elective deferrals
subject to a personal annual limit. As the employer, Jordan makes a
profit-sharing contribution based on self-employment earnings.

For 2026 the regular elective-deferral limit is $24,500 and the age-50 catch-up
limit is $8,000. The regular limit is shared across all 401(k)-type plans an
individual participates in during the year, so the $10,000 outside deferral
counts against the $24,500 regular limit first. The catch-up limit is separate
and is not reduced by the outside deferral because the outside employer made no
catch-up.

The employer side uses an adjusted plan compensation figure. Under the supplied
deterministic model, adjusted plan compensation is Schedule C profit minus half
of the modeled self-employment tax. The employer contribution is 20% of that
adjusted plan compensation, with no additional circular reduction. The 2026
defined-contribution limit is $72,000 of annual additions per participant;
catch-up contributions are not counted against this limit.

A one-participant plan generally files Form 5500-EZ once total plan assets reach
$250,000.

## How we calculated it

**Step 1 — Net earnings subject to self-employment tax.**
$180,000 × 92.35% = **$166,230.00**.

**Step 2 — Modeled self-employment tax.**
$166,230.00 × 15.3% = **$25,433.19**.

**Step 3 — Deductible half of self-employment tax.**
$25,433.19 ÷ 2 = **$12,716.60** (unrounded $12,716.595).

**Step 4 — Adjusted plan compensation.**
$180,000.00 − $12,716.60 = **$167,283.40** (unrounded $167,283.405).
This is Schedule C profit minus half of the modeled self-employment tax. No
further circular reduction is applied under the supplied model.

**Step 5 — Employer (profit-sharing) contribution.**
20% × $167,283.40 = **$33,456.68** (unrounded $33,456.681).
The rate is applied directly to adjusted plan compensation, with no division by
1.20.

**Step 6 — Employee regular deferral remaining.**
$24,500 − $10,000 (outside deferral) = **$14,500.00**.

**Step 7 — Employee age-50 catch-up.**
Jordan is 54, so the full catch-up is available: **$8,000.00**. The outside
employer made no catch-up, so none is used.

**Step 8 — Total employee deferral into the solo plan.**
$14,500.00 + $8,000.00 = **$22,500.00**.

**Step 9 — Annual additions tested against the defined-contribution limit.**
$14,500.00 (regular deferral) + $33,456.68 (employer) = **$47,956.68**.
This is below the $72,000 limit, so the plan is within the limit. The $8,000
catch-up is added on top and is not tested against the $72,000 limit.

**Step 10 — Total modeled 2026 contribution.**
$22,500.00 + $33,456.68 = **$55,956.68**.

## What remains open

- **Plan-document terms.** The plan must allow elective deferrals, catch-up,
  and a 20% profit-sharing contribution. Confirm the available contribution
  types and the adoption deadline with the plan provider or TPA. Elective
  deferrals generally require the plan to be adopted by plan year-end.
- **Outside deferral confirmation.** Confirm with the unrelated employer or
  payroll provider that the $10,000 was a regular elective deferral (not a
  designated Roth or after-tax amount) and that no catch-up was made. This
  controls how much regular deferral room remains.
- **State-law, controlled-group, and other-plan-balance facts.** The model
  assumes no other plan balances and no controlled-group or
  affiliated-service-group issue. Confirm these with the tax adviser, because
  they can change the limits.
- **Self-employment tax model.** The 15.3% combined rate and the ignored Social
  Security wage-base split are an evaluation simplification. A real filing uses
  the actual SE tax computation, which can change the half-of-SE-tax deduction
  and therefore the employer contribution.
- **Employer contribution method.** The supplied model applies 20% directly to
  adjusted plan compensation with no circular reduction. The IRS Publication 560
  deduction worksheet for self-employed individuals uses a circular calculation
  in an actual filing. Confirm with the CPA which method applies before filing.
- **2026 limit refresh.** Confirm the 2026 limits against any later IRS guidance
  before filing. The amounts here use the limits supplied for this evaluation.
- **Filed-result distinction.** These numbers are a planning estimate. The
  final amounts become a filed result only when reflected on the 2026 Form 1040
  and supporting schedules.

### Form 5500-EZ monitoring

A one-participant plan with total assets under $250,000 generally is not
required to file Form 5500-EZ. Once total plan assets reach $250,000 at
year-end, an annual Form 5500-EZ filing is generally required. For a
12/31/2026 plan year-end, the unextended due date is 7/31/2027; Form 5558 can
extend the filing to 10/15/2027. Track the account balance each year-end and
confirm the current due date, extension rules, and threshold with the CPA for
the filing year.

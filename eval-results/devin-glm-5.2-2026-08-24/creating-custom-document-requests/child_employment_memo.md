## 3. Governing 2026 federal rules

1. **Family employees (FICA/FUTA exemptions).** Wages paid by a parent to a
   child under age 18 for work in the parent's sole proprietorship generally are
   **not** subject to Social Security and Medicare (FICA) taxes. Wages to a
   child under age 21 generally are **not** subject to FUTA. Federal
   income-tax withholding and reporting rules still apply. Entity type matters:
   the FICA exemption does **not** apply if the employer is a corporation or
   partnership (other than a partnership in which each partner is a parent of
   the child). Source: IRS, *Family Employees*.

2. **Payroll procedures.** Use current Pub. 15 procedures for withholding
   forms, deposit rules, and Form W-2 reporting. Source: IRS Publication 15.

3. **2026 dependent standard deduction.** For a dependent, the standard
   deduction is the **greater of $1,350 or earned income + $450, capped at
   $16,100**. Source: IRS Rev. Proc. / IRB 2025-45.

4. **2026 IRA/Roth IRA contribution limit.** The annual IRA contribution limit
   for 2026 is **$7,500** (under 50). A Roth IRA contribution is limited to the
   lesser of earned income or the statutory limit, subject to MAGI phase-outs.
   Source: IRS IR-2025-111 / Notice 2025-67.

## 4. Reasonable wage determination

**Comparable analysis.** The three supplied local comparables for substantially
similar entry-level work are $16, $18, and $20 per hour. The median is **$18**.
The client facts direct use of the median unless a documented fact supports
otherwise; no such fact is supplied.

**Reasonable wage (planning estimate).**

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Median comparable hourly rate | $18.00 |
| Documented hours | 160 |
| **Reasonable total wages (2026)** | **$2,880** |

Calculation: 160 × $18 = **$2,880**.

**Owner budget vs. reasonable pay.** The owner's proposed $8,000 budget implies
$8,000 ÷ 160 = **$50/hour**, which is roughly 2.8× the median comparable. Paying
$50/hour for entry-level tagging/organization work would not be defensible as
reasonable compensation for the services actually rendered and would create
reclassification and Schedule C deduction-disallowance risk. Wages must reflect
**actual services** at a rate comparable to what an unrelated entry-level worker
would be paid for the same work. The $8,000 budget is therefore **not** the wage
figure; the reasonable wage is $2,880.

> **Planning estimate vs. filed result.** $2,880 is a planning estimate derived
> from the supplied comparables and documented hours. The filed Schedule C wage
> deduction and Arjun's Form W-2 will reflect the hours actually worked and paid
> during 2026, supported by contemporaneous timesheets.

## 5. Federal payroll tax classification

Arjun is under 18, works in Priya's sole proprietorship, and is Priya's child.
Applying the family-employee rules:

| Payroll tax | Applies? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Social Security tax (6.2% employer + 6.2% employee) | **No — exempt** | Child under 18, sole proprietorship |
| Medicare tax (1.45% employer + 1.45% employee) | **No — exempt** | Child under 18, sole proprietorship |
| FUTA (federal unemployment) | **No — exempt** | Child under 21 |
| Federal income tax withholding | **Yes — applies** | Withholding/reporting rules are not exempted for family employees |
| Form W-2 filing | **Yes — required** | Annual wage reporting required regardless of FICA/FUTA exemption |

**Employer-side impact for Priya.** Because FICA is exempt, Priya pays **no**
employer Social Security or Medicare tax on Arjun's wages and remits **no**
employee FICA. Because FUTA is exempt, no federal unemployment tax applies. The
$2,880 wages remain a deductible Schedule C business expense (ordinary and
necessary compensation for services actually rendered), subject to the
reasonableness standard above.

**Trade-off.** FICA exemption means Arjun earns **no Social Security work
credits** from this employment. This is a known consequence of the family-employee
exemption and should be communicated to the client.

## 8. Documentation-first implementation plan

The plan is **documentation-first**: establish the supporting records before, or
contemporaneously with, paying wages, so the arrangement is defensible if
examined. The controls below map to the records needed to support the facts.

### 8.1 Pre-employment records (before first paycheck)

1. **Written job description.** Define Arjun's duties (product-photography
   tagging, file organization), the age-appropriate scope, and that the work is
   real and necessary to the business — not personal/household labor.
2. **Rate support memorandum.** Document the three comparables ($16, $18, $20),
   the median selection ($18), and the basis for the rate. Retain the source of
   each comparable.
3. **Child-labor law confirmation.** Obtain counsel/payroll-provider
   confirmation that the hours, times, and tasks are lawful for a 15-year-old
   under applicable federal and state child-labor rules (including any work-hour
   and hazardous-occupation restrictions). **Do not begin work until cleared.**
4. **Form W-4.** Collect Arjun's Form W-4 (claim exempt from federal income tax
   withholding if the conditions in Section 6 are met). Retain on file.
5. **Payroll-provider setup.** Confirm with the payroll provider that the
   sole-proprietorship / parent-employer / under-18 FICA exemption and under-21
   FUTA exemption are correctly configured, and that federal income tax
   withholding and W-2 reporting are enabled.

### 8.2 Contemporaneous records (during 2026)

6. **Timesheets.** Record each session: date, start/stop times, task, and hours.
   The 160 documented hours must be substantiated by contemporaneous entries
   signed/initialed by Priya.
7. **Payroll records.** Process wages through the payroll system at $18/hour
   against actual hours, with pay stubs reflecting gross wages, $0 FICA, and
   federal income tax withholding status (exempt or as withheld).
8. **Payment trail.** Pay wages to an account in Arjun's name (not commingled
   with household funds) so the payment is traceable.

### 8.3 Year-end and retention records

9. **Form W-2.** Issue Arjun a 2026 Form W-2 showing $2,880 wages (or actual
   paid amount), with FICA/FUTA boxes reflecting the exemptions. File with
   SSA/IRS per Pub. 15 deadlines.
10. **Schedule C wage deduction.** Report the wages paid as a Schedule C
    business expense, supported by the timesheets, rate memorandum, and W-2.
11. **Roth IRA establishment and contribution.** If proceeding, open a Roth IRA
    for Arjun with a custodian that accepts minor accounts (custodial/guardian
    IRA), confirm plan-document eligibility, and contribute up to the lesser of
    earned income or $7,500 (= $2,880) by the 2026 tax filing deadline.
12. **Retention.** Retain the job description, rate memorandum, timesheets, pay
    stubs, W-4, W-2, and child-labor clearance for the federal recordkeeping
    period (generally at least 4 years for employment tax records; follow the
    tax adviser's retention guidance).

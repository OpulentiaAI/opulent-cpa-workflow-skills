# Worked synthetic example

## Scenario

A physician buys a passenger vehicle for a Schedule C activity. Included basis items total $65,000. The mileage log has 6,000 qualified business miles, 4,000 commuting miles, and 10,000 personal miles. Qualified business use is 30 percent.

The fixture applies the official predominant-use concept for 2025. The sample input also supplies a synthetic 10 percent rate for arithmetic testing. That rate is not drawn from an IRS table.

## Result

- total miles: 20,000
- qualified business use: 30 percent
- more than 50 percent: no
- business basis: $19,500
- fixture deduction at the supplied rate: $1,950
- counterfactual at 100 percent use and the same supplied rate: $6,500

The analysis flags Section 179 and special depreciation allowance as ineligible under the predominant-use test. A production run must confirm every other limit, method, recovery period, convention, and form line for the stated tax year.
